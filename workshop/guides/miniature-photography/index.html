Basic Miniature Photography Setup

A consistent photo station turns finished hobby work into useful documentation, portfolio material, and trustworthy listing images without requiring an expensive camera system.

Written by Stephen Wilkinson Updated August 9, 2026 7 minute read No paid links

01. Minimum photo station

Build one repeatable setup before buying more equipment

A useful miniature photo station needs a stable camera position, a clean background, soft light from more than one direction, and enough room to repeat the same framing. Consistency matters more than an expensive camera.

CameraA phone or camera that can lock focus and exposure.
SupportA tripod, clamp, or rigid stand that does not move between views.
BackgroundA matte paper or card sweep with no visible corner behind the model.
LightingTwo diffused lights placed to reveal form without hard reflections.
ReferenceA small ruler or color-neutral card for scale and repeatability when needed.
Cleaning toolsAir blower, soft brush, and lens cloth for dust and fingerprints.

02. Lighting placement

Use broad, soft light and control reflections

  1. Place the miniature far enough from the background to reduce hard shadows.
  2. Start with a key light above and about 45 degrees to one side.
  3. Add a weaker fill from the opposite side instead of pointing both lights straight at the model.
  4. Diffuse small light sources so glossy armor, metallic paint, and varnish do not become white hotspots.
  5. Turn off mixed room lighting when it creates conflicting color temperatures.
  6. Move the light before increasing editing. The physical highlight pattern should already describe the miniature.
Do not judge color from the screen alone.

Compare the photo to the miniature under the same neutral light. Product photos should describe the object, not create a different paint job.

03. Camera workflow

Stabilize first, then control focus and exposure

  • Clean the lens before every session.
  • Avoid the ultra-wide camera for single miniatures because it exaggerates perspective.
  • Use the normal camera or a true optical telephoto option, then move the camera to frame the model.
  • Use a timer or remote trigger so pressing the shutter does not move the device.
  • Tap or select the face and upper torso as the first focus target.
  • Lower exposure when light armor or metallic highlights are clipping to solid white.
  • Keep the camera height consistent across front, side, and rear views.

04. Required shot list

Capture the views a buyer or future builder actually needs

ViewPurposeKeep consistent
Front three-quarterMain catalog and social imageCamera height, crop, and background margin
Rear three-quarterShows hidden detail and total finish qualityRotation angle and model scale
Left and right profileDocuments silhouette, weapons, and assemblyLens choice and vertical alignment
Detail cropShows texture, stamp depth, surface quality, or paint detailFocus, lighting direction, and honest sharpness

05. Editing and file control

Correct the capture without hiding the product

  1. Crop and straighten every image to a consistent aspect ratio.
  2. Correct white balance using the neutral background or reference card.
  3. Adjust exposure and contrast conservatively so dark recesses and bright edges still retain detail.
  4. Remove sensor dust or background debris, but do not erase print defects or change product geometry.
  5. Export a high-quality master and a smaller web image.
  6. Name files by product, version, view, and sequence, such as test-plate-v1-front-01.webp.

Keep the original capture. A consistent archive makes it possible to compare product revisions, paint progress, and changes in the photo setup over time.
