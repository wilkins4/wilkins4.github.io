My Resin Printing Workflow for Miniatures

The goal is not one magic exposure setting. It is a controlled process that makes failures explainable and successful prints repeatable across the Saturn 4 Ultra and Saturn 8K.

Written by Stephen Wilkinson Updated August 9, 2026 9 minute read No paid links

01. Current setup

Give each printer and material a defined role

The primary resin setup uses an Elegoo Saturn 4 Ultra, with a Saturn 8K available as an older comparison platform. The working miniature resin blend is Elegoo ABS-Like Pro 2 with roughly 10 percent Siraya Tech Tenacious added for more flex in thin weapons, spikes, and gaming pieces.

The ratio is a starting record, not a universal profile.

Resin age, room temperature, pigment, layer height, machine condition, and model geometry can all change the result. Keep one controlled profile and adjust one variable at a time.

Primary printerSaturn 4 Ultra for current miniature production and repeatable tests.
Secondary printerSaturn 8K for comparison, overflow, and known older profiles.
Rigid resinABS-Like Pro 2 provides the main body of the mixture.
Flexible additiveTenacious is added in a recorded ratio to reduce brittle failures.

02. Safety and workspace

Treat uncured resin as a controlled material

Use the current safety data sheet for every resin in the mixture. Keep uncured resin and contaminated tools away from skin, food areas, children, and pets. Use nitrile gloves, eye protection, ventilation appropriate to the workspace, and a washable containment surface.

  • Keep clean tools and contaminated tools physically separated.
  • Label mixed resin with the components, ratio, and date.
  • Do not pour uncured resin or contaminated wash liquid into household drains.
  • Follow local rules and the manufacturer guidance for curing and disposal.
  • Stop and clean a spill before continuing the print workflow.

03. Print workflow

Use the same sequence for every serious print

  1. Define the goal. Decide whether the job is a display model, gaming piece, stamp plate, dimensional test, or support test.
  2. Inspect and mix. Check the vat, release film, build plate, and resin. Mix settled pigment thoroughly without introducing unnecessary bubbles.
  3. Confirm the profile. Match printer, resin batch, layer height, and room conditions to a named slicer profile.
  4. Orient for the weakest feature. Protect faces, weapon tips, thin banners, flat plates, and drainage paths before optimizing speed.
  5. Review supports manually. Auto-supports are a first pass. Inspect islands, long unsupported edges, cups, suction risks, and contact points.
  6. Run a final preflight. Confirm the correct printer, resin profile, plate clearance, file version, and expected print time.
  7. Wash in stages. Remove bulk resin first, use a cleaner final wash, then let the part dry before judging the surface.
  8. Inspect before final cure. Remove supports when appropriate, check for trapped liquid, and reject cracked or hollow parts that cannot be cleaned safely.
  9. Cure only as much as needed. Rotate the part for even exposure and avoid using extra cure time as a substitute for proper washing.
  10. Record the result. Save what worked, what failed, and the next single change to test.

04. Settings record

Document variables before changing them

A good print log is more valuable than a screenshot of someone else's settings. Store the file version and the conditions that actually produced the part.

FieldWhat to recordWhy it matters
Printer and firmwareExact machine and current versionProfiles are not interchangeable between machines.
Resin batchBrand, product, color, mix ratio, and mix datePigment and flexible additives change exposure and strength.
Room conditionsApproximate temperature and major changesCold resin and large temperature swings can change viscosity and reliability.
Exposure profileLayer height, normal exposure, bottom layers, and transitionThis is the baseline for detail and plate adhesion.
Motion profileLift distance, lift speed, retract speed, and rest settingsMotion can cause support failure even when exposure is correct.
OutcomePass, partial, fail, location on plate, and photosPatterns across the plate often expose mechanical issues.

05. Failure triage

Classify the failure before changing settings

SymptomCheck firstAvoid
Nothing on the plateFile, screen test, build plate seating, vat condition, and bottom exposureIncreasing every exposure value at once.
Rafts print, model failsSupport contacts, islands, orientation, suction, and lift behaviorBlaming resin strength before inspecting supports.
Thin details breakOrientation, support removal, cure time, and resin blendOvercuring a brittle part to make it stronger.
Soft or glossy surfaceWash quality, trapped resin, drying time, and cure coverageCuring a part that is still wet with wash liquid.
Repeated failure in one zonePlate level, release film, screen, debris, and local support loadTreating a mechanical pattern as a slicer-only problem.
