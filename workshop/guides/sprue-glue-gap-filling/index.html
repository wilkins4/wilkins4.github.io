Precise Sprue Glue and Gap Filling

The lid brush is useful for broad work, but precision filling improves when a small amount is moved to a controlled work area and applied with the right disposable tool.

Written by Stephen Wilkinson Updated August 9, 2026 8 minute read No paid links

01. Choose the right filler

Sprue glue is best for plastic seams, not every gap

Sprue glue is plastic dissolved in plastic cement. It can chemically join and fill compatible polystyrene parts, then be scraped and sanded like the surrounding kit. It is strong for hairline seams, shallow gaps, and joints that need to become one piece of plastic.

  • Use sprue glue for compatible plastic-to-plastic seams and shallow reconstruction.
  • Use normal plastic cement when the joint already closes cleanly.
  • Use modeling putty for wide gaps, sculpted transitions, or non-plastic surfaces.
  • Use super glue, pinning, or epoxy where the material is metal, resin, or mixed.

02. Tune the mixture

Keep the master jar stable and adjust a small working amount

The main jar can stay thicker for broad filling. For precise work, move a small amount to a disposable surface and tune that working portion instead of repeatedly changing the entire jar.

  1. Use clean polystyrene sprue from the same general type of miniature plastic.
  2. Cut it into small pieces so the solvent can work evenly.
  3. Add a modest amount to a dedicated plastic-cement container and allow time to dissolve.
  4. Stir, seal, and check again later. Do not force a thick lump through a precision tool.
  5. For detail work, decant a small amount and adjust that portion to the required texture.
Use ventilation and keep the container closed when it is not in use.

Plastic cement contains volatile solvent. Follow the product label, keep it away from heat and flames, and do not use food-contact containers or tools.

03. Applicator choice

Match the tool to the amount of material

ApplicatorBest useMain limitation
Frayed lid brushLarge seams, broad texture, and heavy transferToo coarse for controlled placement near finished detail.
Blunt dispensing needleSmall seams, panel gaps, and placing a narrow beadThick material can clog the opening and must not be forced.
Bamboo detail swabMixing, transferring, shaping, and wiping an edgeFibrous tips can leave material behind if overworked.
Disposable syringeOne-session transfer or measured placementSprue glue can cure in the barrel. Do not use it as storage.
Old synthetic brushShort, controlled strokes with a sacrificial toolThe solvent damages brushes, so this is not a reusable fine brush.

04. Apply and cure

Fill the seam without burying the surface

  1. Dry fit first. Correct warped parts, mold lines, and blocked alignment points before adding filler.
  2. Clean the seam. Remove dust, oil, cured glue, and loose plastic that can interrupt the solvent bond.
  3. Place a controlled amount. Pull a narrow bead along the seam instead of painting the entire area.
  4. Let capillary action work. Do not repeatedly brush the joint after the surface starts softening.
  5. Build in layers. A second small application is easier to finish than one oversized mound.
  6. Allow a full cure. The surface can feel dry while the material underneath is still soft.
  7. Scrape before sanding. Use a sharp blade or scraper to remove the high ridge, then refine with sanding sticks or files.
  8. Prime-test important seams. A light primer coat exposes pits and ridges that bare plastic can hide.

05. Common mistakes

Most failures come from too much material or too little cure time

Soft seam after sandingStop. Let the joint cure longer before cutting or sanding again.
Lost armor edgeUse a smaller applicator and protect the edge with controlled scraping.
Needle keeps cloggingThin only the working portion or choose a wider blunt needle.
Gap is still visiblePrime-test it, then add a second thin layer rather than flooding the joint.
Joint is mixed materialSwitch to super glue, epoxy, putty, or pinning. Solvent filler cannot weld metal or cured resin.
Syringe is stuckDispose of it safely. It is a single-session tool for this material, not a storage system.
